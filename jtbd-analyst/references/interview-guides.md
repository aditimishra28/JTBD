# Interview Guides: Jobs Interviews & Switch Interviews

> Distilled from Kalbach, *The Jobs to Be Done Playbook*, Ch. 3. See `sources.md`.

You can't brainstorm jobs or pull them from analytics — you have to interview job performers and translate what you hear into JTBD language. Two interview types, two purposes.

## Jobs interviews (discover the job, process, and needs)

**Recruit job performers, not proxies.** Talk to the people who actually execute the main job — not buyers, procurement, or people who "speak on behalf of" users. You don't need them to know your brand (it can bias them). Use a screening script with clear include/exclude criteria.

- **Sample size:** 5–6 to start seeing patterns; 10–12 is better; 20+ for high confidence.
- **Format:** 1–2 hours, on-site if possible. Two researchers — one interviewer (builds rapport, steers), one observer.
- **Discussion guide, not a questionnaire:** a 1–2 page list of prompts for *you*. Let the conversation flow; use the guide to get back on track.

**Five probe areas:**

1. **Background & the job** — "Tell me about yourself and what you do." "When did you last do [the job]?" "How did it feel overall?"
2. **Main & related jobs** — "What are you trying to accomplish?" "What problems are you trying to prevent?" "What else are you trying to get done?"
3. **Process** — "How do you get started?" "What's the previous/next step?" "How do you decide along the way?" "How do you know you did it right?" "How do you wrap up?"
4. **Needs** — "What workarounds do you use?" "What do you dread or avoid, and why?" "What's the most annoying part?" "What could be easier?"
5. **Circumstances** — "In which situations do you act differently?" "What conditions change your decisions?" "How does the setting affect how you feel?"

**Granularity control:** ask **"why?"** to zoom out toward outcomes; ask **"how?"** to zoom in on process. Sketch the process together and point to steps to dig deeper.

**Critical-incident technique** (gets people off generalities and onto real experience):
1. Recall a specific time the job went particularly wrong.
2. Describe what happened, what went wrong and why, and how they felt.
3. Discuss the ideal — what *should* have happened. This surfaces the underlying need.

**Tips:** build rapport (eye contact, nods); listen more than you talk; never ask people to design solutions (they know their objectives, not your product); hold any product questions until the very end.

## Switch interviews (understand why people changed solutions)

Used in the Switch school to reverse-engineer a real adoption decision. Recruit people who **recently switched** to (or away from) a solution. Reconstruct the purchase as a **timeline** and locate the **Four Forces** along it (see `four-forces.md`):

- Anchor on a concrete, recent decision — "Walk me back to the day you first thought the old way wasn't working."
- Trace: first thought → passive looking → active looking → deciding → first use.
- At each point, surface what pushed them, what pulled them, what made them anxious, and what habits held them back.
- Capture the emotional and social context, the timeline of events, and the moment of commitment.

## Translating interviews into the model

After interviews: extract job statements (verb + object + clarifier), build the job map, write desired-outcome statements, and tag emotional/social jobs and circumstances. Use the templates in `../assets/`.
