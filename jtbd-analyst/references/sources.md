# Sources & Attribution

The methodology in this skill's reference library is **distilled and paraphrased** from the works below. These files are original summaries written for use inside this skill — they are not reproductions of the source texts. For full treatment, definitions, worked examples, and worksheets, consult the primary sources directly.

## Primary source for this skill

- **Jim Kalbach, *The Jobs to Be Done Playbook: Align Your Markets, Organization, and Strategy Around Customer Needs*. Rosenfeld Media / Two Waves Books, 2020.**
  The structure of this reference library — the five elements, the job ecosystem roles, the value-creation cycle, and the play catalog — follows Kalbach's synthesis. Chapters used: Introduction & Ch. 1 (understanding JTBD), Ch. 2 (core concepts), Ch. 3 (discovering value: interviews, four forces, job mapping), Ch. 4 (defining value: underserved needs), Ch. 5–7 (designing, delivering, redeveloping value), and the Quick Reference.

## Foundational and related works cited by Kalbach

- **Clayton M. Christensen** — *The Innovator's Dilemma* (1997) and *The Innovator's Solution* (2003); popularized "jobs to be done" and the "milkshake" framing.
- **Tony Ulwick / Strategyn** — *What Customers Want* (2005) and *Jobs to Be Done: Theory to Practice* (2016); Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI), the desired-outcome statement formula, the universal job map, and the opportunity algorithm.
- **Bob Moesta** — the Switch interview technique and the Four Forces of Progress.
- **Theodore Levitt** — "people want a quarter-inch hole, not a quarter-inch drill" (origin of outcome thinking).
- **Peter Drucker** — *Innovation and Entrepreneurship* (1985); early use of "job to be done."
- **Dan Olsen** — *The Lean Product Playbook* (2015); importance-vs-satisfaction matrix.
- **Scott D. Anthony et al.** — *The Innovator's Guide to Growth* (2008); the jobs scoring sheet.

## How to use within the skill

When a user asks for the source of a claim or wants to go deeper, point them to the relevant work above. Do not paste long passages from any of these texts; summarize and cite.
