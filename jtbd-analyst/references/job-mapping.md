# Job Mapping: The Eight Universal Stages

> Distilled from Kalbach, *The Jobs to Be Done Playbook*, Ch. 3 ("Map the Main Job"), building on Ulwick's universal job map. See `sources.md`.

A **job map** breaks the main job into the sequence of stages a performer moves through — described in terms of *what they're trying to accomplish at each step*, never the solution they use. It is a deliberate abstraction: a stable model of the job, not a literal workflow of one tool.

## The eight universal stages

Every job, at the functional level, can be described with these stages. Not every job emphasizes every stage, but using a consistent set keeps maps comparable.

| # | Stage | The performer is trying to… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Define** | Determine objectives and plan the approach — what does "done" look like? |
| 2 | **Locate** | Gather the inputs, information, and resources needed |
| 3 | **Prepare** | Set up the environment and organize materials |
| 4 | **Confirm** | Verify they're ready and everything is in order before executing |
| 5 | **Execute** | Carry out the core action of the job |
| 6 | **Monitor** | Check that execution is going as intended |
| 7 | **Modify** | Make adjustments and corrections as needed |
| 8 | **Conclude** | Finish the job and wrap up |

## How to build the map

1. **Anchor on the main job** (verb + object). The map describes *this* job at one altitude.
2. **Write each stage as an objective**, solution-free. e.g., for *prepare a meal*: Locate = "acquire ingredients," not "go to the grocery store app."
3. **Populate from evidence.** For each stage, capture what the performer does and the friction observed. If you have no evidence for a stage, mark it *[insufficient data]* rather than inventing it.
4. **Hunt the under-described stages.** Confirm, Monitor, and Modify are the most commonly overlooked — and often where the richest unmet needs hide.
5. **Attach needs to stages.** Each desired-outcome statement (see `needs-and-opportunities.md`) should map to a stage; this is what makes the map actionable.

## Interview moves that build the map

- Ask **"how?"** to move *down* into process detail (more specific steps).
- Ask **"why?"** to move *up* toward outcomes and the broader objective.
- **Sketch the process with the participant** during the interview, then point to steps to probe goals, feelings, and context at each one.

## Prioritizing by stage (alternative to need-level scoring)

Instead of (or before) scoring individual needs, you can survey the **job steps** themselves on importance and satisfaction, then plot them on a 2×2 matrix. This reveals which *part of the process* holds the most strategic opportunity — a faster, coarser-grained version of the opportunity algorithm in `needs-and-opportunities.md`.
