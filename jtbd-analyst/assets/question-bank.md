# Gap-Driven Follow-Up Question Bank

Pick questions that close the **Missing** or **Partial** elements from the transcript extraction (see `../references/transcript-extraction.md`). Ask 3–5, one idea each, only about genuine gaps. Never ask the customer to design a solution.

## Job performer (who actually does the job?)
- Who actually does this day-to-day — is it you, or someone on your team?
- Whose problem is this most — who feels the pain when it goes wrong?
- Are you doing the job here, or buying/overseeing it for someone else?

## Main job (the objective beneath the request)
- Stepping back from the tools — what are you ultimately trying to accomplish?
- If this worked perfectly, what would you have gotten done by the end of it?
- What's the bigger thing this is in service of?

## Process (the job map — esp. Confirm / Monitor / Modify)
- Walk me through it start to finish — what's the very first step, and the last?
- After [step X], how do you know it was done correctly before you move on?
- What happens when something looks off mid-way — how do you catch and fix it?
- How do you decide you're finished and can wrap up?

## Needs (turn complaints into measurable outcomes)
- When you say it's "[their word: slow / hard / annoying]" — what specifically, and what would good enough look like?
- What workarounds have you built to cope with this today?
- What's the single most annoying part, and why is *that* the one that gets you?
- What do you dread or avoid in this process, and why?

## Circumstances (what changes the job)
- In which situations does this go smoothly vs. badly — what's different about the bad ones?
- What conditions — timing, volume, pressure, who's involved — change how you approach it?
- Is it different at month-end / peak / when you're short-staffed?

## Emotional & social jobs
- How do you want to feel while doing this — and how does it feel today?
- Does anyone else see the result of this work? How do you want to come across to them?

## Four Forces (only if switching behavior is present)
- What first made you start looking for a different way of doing this? *(Push)*
- What attracted you to the alternative you're considering? *(Pull)*
- What's giving you pause about changing — what might go wrong? *(Anxiety)*
- What keeps you on the current approach even though it frustrates you? *(Habit)*

## Universal unsticking move (when answers go generic)
**Critical-incident probe:** "Tell me about the last specific time this went wrong — what happened, how did you feel, and what should have happened instead?"

## Anti-patterns — don't ask
- Solution questions ("Would you want a button that…?") — ask about the objective instead.
- Compound questions (two asks in one).
- Anything the transcript already answers.
- Leading questions that put words in their mouth.
